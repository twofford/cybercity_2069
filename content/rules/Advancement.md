# Advancement

Characters get better by earning XP on jobs and spending it in downtime. See [[Character Creation]] for starting characters and [[Rules Reference]] for difficulty tiers.

## Earning XP

At the end of a job, every PC who was on it earns XP for the job's level. The level is the difficulty tier of the job's main obstacle, rated the same way as a roll.

| Job level       | Looks like                         | XP  |
| --------------- | ---------------------------------- | --- |
| Easy            | Rough up a dealer, steal a car     | 1   |
| Medium          | Lift a shipment from a gang        | 2   |
| Hard            | Hit an ordinary corp site          | 3   |
| Very Hard       | A secure corp or government target | 5   |
| Nigh Impossible | A corp's headquarters, a consulate | 8   |

- **A job below your Rep** pays half, rounded up.
- **A failed job** that you walk away from pays half, rounded up. Both halvings together pay a quarter, rounded up.
- **Drive:** +1 XP if you took a real risk for your Drive during the job.
- **Line:** +1 XP if holding your Line cost you something.

## Rep

Rep is how big a job people on the street think you can handle. Every character starts as a **Nobody**.

| Rep    | Matches job level |
| ------ | ----------------- |
| Nobody | Easy              |
| Local  | Medium            |
| Known  | Hard              |
| Wanted | Very Hard         |
| Legend | Nigh Impossible   |

- **Finish a job above your Rep** and your Rep rises one step.
- **Jobs below your Rep** pay half XP (see above). To keep growing, a crew has to take bigger work.

## Spending XP

Spend XP only in downtime, somewhere safe.

- **Raise a skill:** costs XP equal to the new rating. 2 → 3 costs 3; 4 → 5 costs 5. Skills cap at 5.
- **Raise a stat:** costs three times the new rating. 1 → 2 costs 6; 2 → 3 costs 9. Stats cap at 3.

You can't spend XP on HP, chrome or gear. Once a character tops out, they grow through money, chrome and contacts.
